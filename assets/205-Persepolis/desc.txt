Persepolis 2005


This photograph was taken just outside the ruins of Persepolis in southern Iran. Behind me stood two-and-a-half-thousand years of history, and in front of me—a newly built car park with young trees. While everyone else pointed their cameras toward the ruins, I looked ridiculous pointing mine the other way. After all, the ruins have been photographed millions of times, but I think this picture is unique.

Shot on film, this is a fully analog image with no digital manipulation. Everything you see was created in-camera through exposure, depth of field, and film speed.

Although available on canvas, this piece works especially well as a Giclée Hahnemühle Photo Rag print. It’s a more expensive paper, but worth it, the image truly comes to life on its surface. The super-matte finish enhances muted blacks, preserves fine detail, and offers even colour reproduction. Its subtle texture and smooth, cotton feel create beautifully soft colours.
