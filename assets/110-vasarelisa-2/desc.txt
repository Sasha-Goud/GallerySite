Binomial, 1984

As a teenager of the 1960s, I was fascinated by geometric art and often created them in various media. When I later discovered the work of Victor Vasarely and M. C. Escher, I realised that everything I was exploring had already been done brilliantly and completely. That realisation stopped me from pursuing it further as a visual art form.

Instead, I began incorporating those ideas into photography. This image is part of that evolution. It’s a projection of one of Escher’s works onto the human body. The photograph plays with light, void, and form, asking the viewer’s brain to complete what’s left out. After many experiments in the 1970s, I finally captured the image as I imagined it in 1984.
